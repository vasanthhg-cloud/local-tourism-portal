Discover the district

A gateway to Karnataka's quiet interior.

Tumakuru (Tumkur) sits at the foothills of the Devarayanadurga ranges, about 70 km from Bengaluru. It is a district of dramatic monoliths, hill forts, ancient temples and a spiritual legacy that still shapes everyday life — from the discipline of Siddaganga Mutt to the Ramayana legend of Namada Chilume.

Long known as an industrial town, Tumakuru is equally a traveller's district: forested hills for trekking, heritage temples for the curious, bird habitats and reservoirs for slow weekends, and warm village hospitality for those who stay a while. This portal brings the local side of that travel together in one place.

Getting there

How to reach Tumakuru

By rail

Tumakuru & Kyatasandra stationsRegular trains from Bengaluru and Arsikere; Kyatasandra is the stop closest to Siddaganga Mutt.

By air

Kempegowda Intl. (BLR)The nearest airport, roughly 90 km away — hire a cab or take a train from Bengaluru.

Best time

October – FebruaryPleasant weather for fort climbs, temple visits and hill drives; summer afternoons can be hot.
01Madhugiri FortHistoric · Madhugiri taluk
History & legend

Madhugiri takes its name from madhu-giri, “honey hill”, for the bee colonies that once dotted its northern slopes. The fort was begun around 1670 by Raja Hire Gowda, a local chief. The story goes that a stray sheep came home one night dripping wet, hinting at a hidden spring — a welcome sign in a rain-starved land, and reason enough to fortify the hill. It later passed through the hands of the Mysore Wodeyars and Haider Ali.

What to see

One of Asia’s largest monoliths, climbed through a chain of gateways — Antaralada Bagilu, Diddi Bagilu and the Mysuru Gate — past old granaries, a Jain Mallinatha Basti and, at the summit, the ruined Gopalakrishna temple. The town below is known for its pomegranates.

Best time & getting there

October to February is ideal. About 43 km north of Tumakuru; allow three to four hours for the climb, and start early — the bare rock gets hot by midday.

02DevarayanadurgaSpiritual · 12 km from Tumakuru
History & legend

A hill retreat whose temples were built and renovated by the Mysore rulers in the Dravidian style. It sits inside the Devarayanadurga State Forest, and the rivers Jayamangali and Shimsha rise in these hills.

What to see

The Bhoga Narasimha temple at the foot of the hill and the Yoga Narasimha temple higher up; the Sanjivaraya (Hanuman) and Kumbhi Narasimha shrines; three sacred ponds — Narasimha Teertha, Parashara Teertha and Pada Teertha, the last inside a cave; and a cave holding images of Rama, Sita and Lakshmana.

Best time & getting there

Cool even in summer, and at its best from October to February. About 12–14 km from Tumakuru and roughly 73 km from Bengaluru.

03Siddaganga MuttSpiritual · Tumakuru city
History & legend

An ancient gurukul and Veerashaiva matha, and one of Karnataka’s most respected spiritual and educational institutions. It runs on the principle of dasoha — that no one who comes should go unfed — and its kitchen has served free meals to students and pilgrims for generations.

What to see

The Siddhalingeshwara temple on a hillock, with six shrines at its entrance; the surrounding campus of schools and hostels; and the daily community dining that is central to the mutt’s life.

Best time & getting there

Open through the year, about 6 AM to 9 PM. In Tumakuru city; Kyatasandra is the nearest railway stop.

04Namada ChilumeNature · 14 km from Tumakuru
History & legend

The name means “spring of the tilak”. In the Ramayana tradition, Rama, in exile with Sita and Lakshmana, needed water to apply his forehead mark; finding none, he shot an arrow into the rock and a spring burst out. It has never dried, even in the worst droughts.

What to see

The spring itself, issuing from a hole in the rock bed; the surrounding forest; a small deer park; and a travellers’ bungalow for those who want to stay a while.

Best time & getting there

Morning is the best time, and the deer park stays closed on Mondays. On the Devarayanadurga road, about 14 km from Tumakuru.

05ShivagangeTrek · Gangenahalli
History & legend

A steep granite hill crowned by a temple, long a place of pilgrimage and, more recently, a favourite weekend trek from Bengaluru. Shrines and springs mark the route to the top.

What to see

The climb past shrines and a monolith Nandi to the summit temple; wide views over the plains; and the Olakal Teertha spring on the way up.

Best time & getting there

Early morning from October to February. About 30 km from Devarayanadurga; the climb takes roughly an hour and a half to two hours.

History & legend

A granite hill sacred to the Jains, its name a contraction of “mandara-giri”. A temple with a striking blue dome crowns the summit, reached by a long flight of stone steps.

What to see

The summit temple and its dome; sunrise and sunset views; and the quiet countryside around Pandithanahalli.

Best time & getting there

Sunrise is the classic time to climb. About 19 km from Devarayanadurga.

07Channarayana DurgaTrek · near Madhugiri
History & legend

A hill fortress at about 3,734 ft, built in the 17th century by Channapa Gouda, a feudal lord of Madhugiri. It later passed to the Mysore kingdom and to the Marathas, and many battles were fought for its possession. Its slopes are rich in ayurvedic herbs, which earned it the name Siddharabetta.

What to see

The fort ruins and gateways; the Murarimatha shrine at the foot of the hill; and broad views from the top.

Best time & getting there

October to February, starting early. A moderate half-day trek near Madhugiri.

08Yediyur Siddhalingeshwara TempleSpiritual · Kunigal taluk
History & legend

A pilgrimage temple in Yediyur village, built around the shrine of the venerated saint Tontada Siddalinga, who lived in the late 15th century.

What to see

The saint’s shrine and the temple’s tiered gopura; the annual car festival on the seventh lunar day of Chaitra (March–April); and the lamps that fill the temple at Deepavali.

Best time & getting there

Maha Shivratri, Ugadi and the Chaitra car festival draw the largest crowds. About 60 km from Tumakuru.

09Goravanahalli Mahalakshmi TempleSpiritual · Koratagere taluk
History & legend

One of the district’s best-known temples, dedicated to Goddess Mahalakshmi, whose idol is held to be self-originated. The temple offers free lunch and prasadam to visitors each day.

What to see

The sanctum of Mahalakshmi, along with shrines to Marikamba and Manchala Nagappa; the green surroundings; and the nearby Theetha reservoir.

Best time & getting there

Fridays, when special pooja is performed, and during festival days. About 30 km from Tumakuru and roughly 90 km from Bengaluru.

10Seebi Narasimha Swamy TempleHeritage · Sira taluk
History & legend

Built around 1797 by Fauzdar Nallappa, son of a dewan in Tipu Sultan’s court, after a dream in which the god Narasimha promised him happiness if a temple were raised on the spot. Construction took about ten years.

What to see

The Dravidian-style temple and its celebrated 18th-century terracotta panels and frescoes.

Best time & getting there

Open through the day; October to February is the most comfortable. On NH-4, about 20 km north of Tumakuru.

View on map
11Kaidala & Aralaguppe — Hoysala stoneworkHeritage · Gubbi & Tiptur taluks
History & legend

Kaidala is the birthplace of Jakanachari, the master sculptor of Hoysala legend, and its Chennakeshava temple dates to about 1150 AD. At Aralaguppe, the Hoysala Chennakeshava of about 1250 AD stands beside a 9th-century Kalleshwara temple in the Ganga-Nolamba style.

What to see

Kaidala’s finely carved Chennakeshava and Gangadhareshwara temples; and at Aralaguppe, the Chennakeshava with its magnificent Vishnu image, plus the Kalleshwara ceiling carved with a dancing Shiva and the eight Dikpalas.

Best time & getting there

Any morning — both are small village temples. Kaidala is about 9 km from Tumakuru; Aralaguppe about 6 km from Banasandra railway station.

12Markonahalli Dam & KaggaladuNature · Kunigal & Turuvekere
History & legend

The Markonahalli dam across the Shimsha river was built during the reign of Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV. Kaggaladu, in the same part of the district, is one of South Asia’s largest painted-stork nesting colonies.

What to see

Lakeside calm, birdwatching and sunsets at Markonahalli; and the stork colony at Kaggaladu, best watched from a respectful distance, especially in nesting season.

Best time & getting there

The winter months are best for birds; early morning or evening for both places.

16Tumakuru City & SiraHistoric · district headquarters
About this place

Tumakuru is the district headquarters; Sira, to its north, was once a provincial capital under the Bijapur and Mughal governors, and still holds a fort and old mosques. Together they make a good base for markets, eateries and the Siddaganga precinct.

Best time & getting there

Through the year, with pleasant mornings and evenings. Tumakuru is about 70 km from Bengaluru, with Sira a further 50 km north.

About this place

A temple town with two Hoysala-age temples — the Chennakeshava (Channigaraya) and Moole Shankareshwara — raised in the 13th century under Narasimha III by the commander Somanna. The Chennakeshava is a protected monument.

Best time & getting there

Any morning. About 60 km from Tumakuru, on the road towards Tiptur.

18Pavagada Fort & Solar ParkHistoric · Pavagada taluk
About this place

Pavagada's fort, said to date from the 15th century, sits on a hill above the town and saw fighting in the Mysore–British wars. The same taluk now holds one of the largest solar parks in the world, spread across thousands of acres.

Best time & getting there

October to February, in daylight. About 100 km from Tumakuru; the fort is a short climb and the solar park is best seen on a drive.

Also known as the Maidenahalli reserve, this is a small protected patch of dry grassland in Madhugiri taluk, kept for its blackbuck herds. The plains also draw harriers, larks and other open-country birds.

Dawn and dusk, in the cooler months. About 60 km from Tumakuru; go with a local guide and keep a respectful distance from the herds.
